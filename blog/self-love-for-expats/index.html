Self Love When You Live Abroad: Why It Can Feel Harder as an Expat

By Danielle Leite · 20 October 2026 · 5 min read

Self love for expats is rarely as simple as the advice makes it sound. A bubble bath and a few affirmations do not go far when you are managing a new language, unfamiliar paperwork and a life in which few people yet know who you really are.

If you have moved abroad and find yourself being harder on yourself than you ever were at home, you are not doing it wrong. This post looks at why self love can feel out of reach after a move, what it actually means, and how a person centred approach can help you build a kinder relationship with yourself, wherever you are living.

Why self love gets harder after a move abroad

A move takes away many of the things that quietly held your sense of self in place: familiar routines, a professional identity people recognised, friends who know your history, and often the language in which you express yourself best. Without them, it is common for the inner critic to grow louder.

Small mistakes can feel magnified when you are doing everything in a second language. You may compare yourself with people who seem settled, or with friends back home whose lives have carried on without you. There can also be an unspoken pressure to be grateful, since you chose this life, which leaves little room to admit that parts of it are hard.

None of this means the move was a mistake. It means you are adjusting, and that being kind to yourself matters more now than it did before.

What self love is, and what it is not

Self love is not constant positivity, and it is not self indulgence. It does not mean pretending that everything is fine, or lowering your standards. It is a way of relating to yourself that includes honesty, acceptance and care, especially when things are not going well.

A useful test is to ask how you would respond if a close friend told you what you are telling yourself. Most of us offer friends patience and understanding, while holding ourselves to a much harsher standard. Self love begins with noticing that gap.

It also differs from self esteem. Self esteem is usually about how you judge yourself, which can rise and fall with your achievements. Self love is more about how you treat yourself, and it can stay steady even in a difficult season.

A person centred view: warmth turned inwards

In person centred counselling, which draws on the work of Carl Rogers, the starting point is that people tend to grow when they are met with warmth, genuine understanding and acceptance, without conditions. Rogers called this unconditional positive regard.

The inner critic works the other way round. It makes your worth conditional on performing well, adapting quickly, or being the person you think you should be by now. Rogers observed that acceptance tends to come before change rather than after it. When you stop fighting who you are today, you often have more energy to move towards who you want to become.

Seen this way, self love is not a mood to be achieved. It is a practice of offering yourself the same acceptance a good counsellor, or a good friend, would offer you.

Four gentle ways to practise self love while living abroad

  1. Name what you miss. Even a move you chose involves loss. Allowing yourself to say so, without justifying it, is a first act of self acceptance.
  2. Speak to yourself as you would to a friend. When you notice a harsh thought, ask what you would say to someone you care about in the same situation, and say that instead.
  3. Build small anchors. A regular walk, a favourite cafe or a weekly call with someone who knows you gives your days a shape that is yours, in a place that is still becoming familiar.
  4. Let yourself be known. Find one or two people, in person or online, with whom you can be honest about how the move really feels. Being understood is one of the quickest routes to feeling worthy of care.

Frequently asked questions

What does self love mean for expats?

For expats, self love means treating yourself with acceptance and care while you adjust to a new country, rather than judging yourself for finding the transition hard.

Why do I feel worse about myself since moving abroad?

A move removes familiar routines, relationships and roles that supported your sense of self. Feeling unsettled for a time is a common response, and it does not mean you made the wrong choice.

Is self love the same as self esteem?

Not quite. Self esteem is how you evaluate yourself, and it can change with circumstances. Self love is how you treat yourself, and it can remain steady when life is difficult.

Can online counselling help with life abroad?

Many people find it helpful to talk with a counsellor who understands expat life, and online sessions mean you can do so from wherever you live, without needing to find someone locally.
